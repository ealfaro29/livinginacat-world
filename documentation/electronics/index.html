Electronics mount

The DLC32, MOSFET driver and cutoff-switch holder, with installation and insulation notes.

CAD prototype; electronics support print completion confirmed by the owner on November 16. On November 17 the owner confirms mounting it and reports an uneven underside. A two-piece cosmetic plate is prepared separately, without clips, holes or changes to this original support; the logo variant in nominal Color Mix 90% yellow and 10% black was observed starting at 0/12 layers. Do not print both PCB holders.

Historical preparation, November 15, 2026: one original electronics support was saved and sliced in Generic PETG, main 0.4 mm, 0.20 mm nominal layers, six walls, six top/bottom layers, 60% gyroid, 8 mm outer brim and removable tree supports from the bed. First-layer, base/posts, guard layers and complete preview were reviewed. Estimates: 4 h 1 min 53 s, 59.57 g and 19.50 m. That PETG preparation was not sent by the agent. The later observed PLA print is described below. The retainer is already included in the preceding eight-piece batch and is not duplicated here. Completed print, owner report of November 16: the owner confirms all project prints complete. The preceding observation recorded the electronics support running and the owner preference for PLA after A2 yellow PLA was observed. The saved current-session copy uses Generic PLA High Speed; the device reports 258 layers, an estimate of 3 h 47 min 26 s and 60.00 g. The earlier PETG files remain preparation history. The job was observed Printing at 8%, layer 1/258. No print command, cancellation, pause or device-setting change was sent by this review. Switch clearance and physical fit remain for the first printed support before final assembly.

The wing faces outside the frame. It carries Adafruit 5648 and NKK S301 with an open-top side/rear low-voltage terminal guard. This is not a closed enclosure or a mains enclosure. Reuse the four DLC32 M3×10 and two frame FHCS M5×12 already counted. Retainer and strain relief use four 4-inch Fermerry ties, with no additional screws.

The switch on V1 is an NKK S301T with screw terminals and an M12 mounting bushing. The original CAD checks used the S301 with solder lugs. Fit the actual switch to the printed panel and check the space around both terminals with their insulation in place before securing the harness.

  1. Use the completed electronics support; remove remaining brim/supports from the mounting surfaces and test fit before installing electronics. No duplicate print is required.
  2. Seat the 5648 on four supports. Two Ø2.1 mm locating pins enter Ø2.5 mm holes at 22.86/2.54 and 22.86/15.24 in the official Eagle coordinates. PCB underside is 2 mm above the platform.
  3. The retainer contacts only two bare mounting ears. Use two ties around outer posts/slots, never over components. Two other ties strain-relieve the JST cable and switch harness. Trim tails with the kit’s cutters.
  4. Fit the S301T in the 3 mm panel, Ø12.7 mm hole and Ø3.2 anti-rotation feature 9 mm from center. Use supplied nuts/washers and an adjustable wrench. Hold the rear nut without rotating the switch body. Verify clearance around both screw terminals and their insulated wires on the first printed holder before installation. Do not apply a metal-panel datasheet maximum torque to printed plastic; check for crushing and loosening.
  5. With all power disconnected, verify the S301T screw connections and wire terminations. Insulate each terminal separately and check the clearance to the guard. The assembly guide shows the mounting sequence.
  6. Keep connectors, microSD and tie slots clear. Verify polarity, insulation, continuity, 12 V cutoff and strain relief before energizing. No exposed copper may contact the profiles or board.

CAD checks: each part is a valid solid and closed, consistently oriented, single-component STL at Z0; STEP/STL volume comparison below 0.2%. Eight intersection checks report zero volume: DLC32/mount, 5648/mount, 5648/retainer, components/retainer, mount/retainer, S301/mount, bushing/panel and S301/5648. DLC32 placements are applied to child solids to avoid compound-transform errors. See qa/electronics.json. These results do not validate wiring or physical fit.
