Original coupler The owner reported a tight fit. The separate REV01 pair was later fitted and checked with power off.

The test material was PLA Silk with a 0.4 mm nozzle. The owner did not itemize results for each round hole, square-nut pocket or countersunk seat, and no measured clearance diameters were supplied. The report therefore does not establish a chosen compensation value or validate every individual feature.

On November 14 the owner confirmed that the second batch — one phone bed and four feet — finished with a good print outcome. The exact completion time and measured fit were not supplied. The owner also selected PLA Silk for the final parts. That material choice was subsequently updated to PETG for the project batch on November 15; neither choice establishes strength, creep or thermal performance.

On November 14 (Costa Rica), the owner confirms the motor-mount A, motor-mount B and belt-clamp batch is printed. Bambu Studio separately shows the named job Finished at 100%, 299/299 layers. Exact completion time and measured fit were not supplied. The earlier 90-layer preparation and its estimates remain historical; they are not the actual finished job record.

The fourth plate contains one solenoid cradle and two clamp bars, in PLA Silk with the main 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.20 mm layers, six walls, six top/bottom layers and 80% gyroid. It was observed starting with Homing toolhead at 0/145 layers. Slicer estimates are 1 h 10 min and 16.35 g; completion and actual consumption remain unconfirmed. Its complete plate overview was reviewed; an individual first-layer/cavity-roof inspection was not completed before the observed send.

On November 15 (Costa Rica), a fifth plate is prepared and sliced: one original tube holder and one Adafruit 5648 retainer. It uses PLA Silk, the main 0.4 mm nozzle, 0.20 mm layers, six walls, six top/bottom layers, 80% gyroid and an 8 mm outer brim. Removable supports are enabled and present in the slice. The final estimates are 52 min 53 s and 6.32 g, not measured duration or consumption. At that preparation cut the files had not been sent to the printer. The fourth batch still has no confirmed completion or measured-fit result.

On November 15 the owner confirmed that the actual M4 screws and nuts fit. No measured clearances or individual hole, pocket or seat results were supplied. At the owner's request, the fifth plate was expanded to eight pieces: the tube holder, Adafruit 5648 retainer, two front idler mounts, two motor-side idler mounts and the left/right XY joints. Original geometries, scale and manifest quantities are verified. The prior two-piece preparation is preserved. The revised plate keeps PLA Silk, main 0.4 mm, 0.20 mm nominal layers, six walls, six top/bottom layers, 80% gyroid, an 8 mm brim and removable tree supports. Its exported slice estimates 4 h 8 min 20 s, 64.17 g and 21.51 m. These are estimates; at that preparation cut the revised plate had not been sent to print. The large electronics holder remains pending S301T clearance verification.

Later on November 15, the owner selected PETG for the project pieces and reported starting the eight-piece batch. Bambu Studio shows the matching job active at 0/442 layers, initially in bed-surface detection and then changing filament. The current project uses Generic PETG on the main 0.4 mm nozzle; its saved PETG project and exported slice preserve all eight original geometries and quantities. Slice estimates are 4 h 26 min 8 s, 63.71 g and 20.86 m. The device job retains its earlier Silk filename, which is separate from the observed PETG profile and owner material choice. The saved PETG session has not been byte-matched to the printer file. Completion, PETG fit and thermal/load performance remain unconfirmed. The previous Silk preparations and the fourth batch's unknown completion are preserved. No new print command or printer-setting change was made during this record update.

On November 15, the next plate contains one original electronics support, prepared and sliced in Generic PETG on the main 0.4 mm nozzle. It uses 0.20 mm nominal layers, six walls, six top/bottom layers, 60% gyroid, an 8 mm outer brim and removable tree supports from the bed. The native project and exported slice preserve the original geometry, scale and quantity. The first layer, base/posts, switch-guard layers and complete slice were reviewed. The 258-layer slice estimates 4 h 1 min 53 s, 59.57 g and 19.50 m; these are not measured results. This plate has not been sent to print. The received switch has screw terminals, while the CAD guard was checked against the earlier solder-lug reference. Assess clearance with the first printed support before final assembly; the owner has not confirmed that fit. No physical check is required before this first support is printed. The earlier eight-piece PETG job was observed printing at 90%, 315/442 layers; completion remains unconfirmed.

On November 15, the owner reports the electronics support is printing and, after the actual material was explained, confirms a preference for PLA. The matching live job uses A2 yellow PLA at an observed 220 °C nozzle and 55 °C bed; the preserved active session uses Generic PLA High Speed. These are observations, not new temperature recommendations. The device filename still contains PETG; the original PETG project and exported slice remain unchanged and are not claimed to be the current printer file. The job was observed Printing at 8%, layer 1/258, with estimates of 3 h 47 min 26 s and 60.00 g. Completion and fit remain unconfirmed. The native home-page saved print history also reports Success for the exact solenoid-cradle/two-bar job; actual completion time, quality and measured fit were not supplied. At that review the eight-piece PETG batch still lacked a completion report. The owner subsequently confirms eight finished pieces: one tiny piece, three pairs and one long piece. This matches the expected prepared plate and confirms completion and count, without independently verifying every individual part, print quality or measured fit. All 22 installed pieces have prepared files: 21 have recorded completion and the electronics support remains printing. Dry assembly can start with the squared frame, feet and rail alignment; wiring and powered tests still require their separate checks.

On November 16, the owner confirms all project prints complete and asks to begin assembly. This records completion of the previously active electronics support: all 22 installed printed pieces now have completion recorded. The exact completion date, quality and measured fit were not supplied. Earlier printer observations and estimates remain historical; no additional print was sent and no material was reclassified. Start with the dry frame and confirm each mechanical checkpoint physically.

At the end of the print batches, the coupler fit, motion, touch accuracy and thermal behavior still required physical checks. The Silk coupons and the completed PETG batch provide separate evidence. Tests on the assembled V1 are recorded below.

A subsequent November 16 owner report confirms the frame with four corner brackets and its feet assembled, with two loose nuts reserved in each long side’s inner lower slot. This is owner evidence, without supplied diagonal measurements or independent visual inspection. Bed crossmembers, rails and full assembly remain unconfirmed. With the bed crossmembers’ slot facing up, their section is 10 mm high and 20 mm wide; the guide’s earlier reversed orientation has been corrected.

Subsequent owner checkpoints confirm the bed and crossmembers, motors and idlers, both long rails, the bridge and short rail, and both belts installed and tensioned. The owner reports smooth full manual travel after tightening the right rail. No powered motion, measured belt tension or complete assembly acceptance is established by those reports.

On November 17, the reported inability to seat the original L-shaped cradle was reproduced against the authentic belt-clamp STEP. Its two ribs intersect the clamp even in the correct six-hole mounting orientation. Cradle REV01 moves each rib 3 mm inward without changing the six mounting holes, four nut cavities or exterior size. Digital interference checks with the clamp, conservative carriage-screw-head envelopes and belt-channel envelopes pass; the STL is watertight and connected. The replacement has not been sliced, printed or physically fitted. The original cradle installation is blocked; the prior completed-print record is preserved. No printer action was performed.

The owner subsequently reports the solenoid installed with both bars and four screws. This does not identify the printed cradle revision or establish measured return, clearance or load performance. The original coupler pair was reported awkward to fit and apparently oval around the tip; printed roundness was not independently measured. The owner measured an 8.83 mm diameter × 4.55 mm high metal collar and 7.90 mm mesh. The original CAD exit is 7.40 mm. Coupler REV01 uses a 9.30 × 4.95 mm collar recess and 8.30 mm mesh exit, preserving the lower shoulder, cap socket and fasteners. CAD circles and uniform-scale meshes pass digital checks. The requested 0.16 mm pair was saved, natively sliced and explicitly authorized to print; the matching active job was observed in initial preparation at 0/48 layers. The owner subsequently confirms both replacement halves printed and installed, and reports completing the unpowered retention/return check before requesting continuation. Actual event dates, measured printed roundness and powered or thermal performance were not supplied. Closing the designed 0.50 mm split gap fully narrows the bore in that direction; this is a possible assembly effect, not a measured explanation of the owner's earlier print.
